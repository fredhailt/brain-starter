# Business ideas AI is genuinely good at helping run

Ranked for a beginner with $5,000 to $10,000, a full-time job, and a strong phone game. Checked 2026-11-12.

**How it is ranked:** least cash at risk, fits evenings and lunch hours, rewards being good on the phone, and the AI can do most of the grind. Start costs are our estimates unless a source is named; check each tool's pricing page before you pay.

## Before any of them: the day job

- Personal Claude account, personal email, personal phone line, personal laptop time. Never company data, the work CRM, the work ZoomInfo seat, or work hours.
- California makes most non-compete clauses void (Business and Professions Code 16600), but your confidentiality and invention terms still apply. Read your own employment agreement. Never sell to your employer's customers or in your employer's market. Advisory only; ask an employment lawyer if your agreement is unclear.

## How they stack

- **1, 2 and 4 sell to the same contractors.** The free audit opens the door, missed-call text-back is the first monthly fee, review follow-up is the second. Build each one once as a skill; the second client is mostly a copy with a new name, number and hours, and the price does not drop because the work did.
- **3, 5 and 7 share the same calls and stock.** The brands you call for wholesale often need listing help, and Amazon leftovers become eBay listings.
- **6 is the slow, big one.** It pays the most once it works and takes the longest to start.

## 1. Free "what does AI say about your business" audit for local contractors

**What it is.** You ask an AI assistant the questions a homeowner asks ("best roofer near me", "how much does a roof repair cost in <town>", "is <company> licensed"), record whether the contractor shows up and whether what the AI says is **true**, and check the contractor's own website for the facts an AI needs. One page, free, sent by text. The finding is the pitch; the fixes are the paid work.

**The facts an AI needs, on the contractor's own page in plain text:** a price or price range, the named service area, license number and insurance, brands and certifications, an FAQ, financing, warranty, how to book with a phone number, and response time. A fact that is missing is the product.

- **Start cost:** your Claude plan. Nothing else.
- **Hours a week:** 3 to 6. Once it is a saved skill, each audit is a short run plus your read.
- **AI does:** runs the questions with web search, reads the site, writes the one-pager, then drafts the fix pages (price ranges, service-area page, FAQ, license line).
- **You do:** pick the targets, make the call, read every report before it goes out, close the paid fix.
- **Honest risk:** AI assistants are still a small slice of web visits: about 0.32% of all website traffic in [SE Ranking's 2026 study](https://seranking.com/blog/ai-traffic-research-study/), about 0.28% in [Ahrefs' March 2026 data](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic/). Say that out loud on the call. The honest pitch: the fixes that make a business clear to an AI (facts in plain text, a fast page, one consistent price) are the same fixes that help in Google, so the contractor buys one piece of work that pays twice. Never sell "llms.txt" files or hidden markup as a magic fix. Your audit runs on one assistant; never say "ChatGPT says" unless you ran it in ChatGPT.
- **First step this week:** pick one trade in your area, run the audit on 3 contractors, read the 3 reports, and call the one with the worst finding.

## 2. Missed-call text-back and an AI receptionist for contractors

**What it is.** When a contractor misses a call, the caller gets a text within a minute ("Sorry we missed you, what do you need help with?") and the reply lands on the contractor's phone. The step up is an AI voice agent that answers after hours, takes the job details, and texts the owner. Contractors on a roof cannot answer the phone; every missed call can be a lost job.

- **Start cost:** a phone and texting platform per client (many small-business CRM tools include missed-call text-back; check their pricing pages), plus carrier registration. US business texting from a local number needs **A2P 10DLC registration** with The Campaign Registry, or carriers filter the texts ([vendor guides, 2026](https://www.dailystory.com/blog/10dlc-registration-for-sms-marketing/); your texting provider walks you through it).
- **Hours a week:** 3 to 5 for the first clients, less once it is a copy-and-change setup.
- **AI does:** writes the text flows and the voice script, builds the setup checklist, reads the call logs, and sends the owner a weekly count of missed calls caught.
- **You do:** sell it, set up accounts in the **client's** name with the client typing their own logins, approve every script.
- **Honest risk:** a crowded market, and contractors cancel if they don't see jobs, so report the jobs caught every week. The law: texting someone right after they called you is the safe case. Texting old customer lists or making outbound calls with an AI voice is regulated: the FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices count as "artificial" voices under the TCPA, which needs prior consent ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-makes-ai-generated-voices-robocalls-illegal)). California requires every party's consent to record a call (Penal Code 632), so any recording needs a "this call may be recorded" greeting. Advisory only; check with a lawyer before you sell outbound anything.
- **First step this week:** call 10 local contractors after 5 PM and count how many go to voicemail. That count is your opening line.

## 3. Amazon wholesale (FBA)

**What it is.** Buy name-brand products in bulk straight from the brand and sell them on existing Amazon listings while Amazon stores and ships them. The full kit is the `amazon/` folder in this repo.

- **Start cost:** Amazon's Professional plan is $39.99 a month ([sell.amazon.com/pricing](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing), checked 2026-11-12), Keepa's paid plan about €29 a month (2026 reviews; check keepa.com), and your first orders. Your $5,000 to $10,000 is the inventory money.
- **Hours a week:** 5 to 15 at the start (research, calls, first shipments), then steadier.
- **AI does:** product research, the price list check, PO drafts, restock forecasts, claims hunting, bookkeeping drafts (see `amazon/06-ai-captains-for-amazon.md`).
- **You do:** the brand calls (most beginners quit here; you won't), every approval, every payment, every click inside Seller Central.
- **Honest risk:** cash is tied up for one to three months; prices can drop; fees change; brands say no a lot; an account problem stops sales. No browser bot inside Seller Central, ever.
- **First step this week:** `amazon/README.md`, week 1: setup and 10 product checks, no money.

## 4. Review requests and past-customer follow-up

**What it is.** After every finished job, an automatic text or email asks the customer for an honest review. Twice a year, past customers get a useful check-in ("time for your furnace tune-up?"). Most small contractors never ask, and the ask alone moves reviews and repeat work.

- **Start cost:** low; it often runs on the same platform as idea 2.
- **Hours a week:** 2 to 4 once set up.
- **AI does:** writes the messages and timing, keeps the opt-out list clean, reports reviews gained and repeat jobs booked.
- **You do:** sell it, make sure the client has customer consent to text, approve every message.
- **Honest risk:** this is where people break rules. Never offer anything for a review and never ask only the happy customers: Google's review policy bans incentives and selective asking ([Google](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)). The FTC's consumer reviews rule (in force since 2024-10-21) bans fake reviews and rewards tied to a positive review, with civil penalties per violation ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). Texting past customers needs their consent.
- **First step this week:** offer it free for 30 days to your first audit client, and count the reviews.

## 5. Listing fixes for small brands

**What it is.** Many small brands you call for wholesale have weak Amazon listings: thin titles, missing details, poor images. Sell a fixed-price fix: a rewritten title and bullet points, a keyword list, and a photo brief. The brand uploads it, or adds you as a user in its own Seller Central where you make the edits by hand.

- **Start cost:** your Claude plan.
- **Hours a week:** 2 to 5.
- **AI does:** drafts the copy from the product and the competing listings, builds the keyword list from public pages, checks it against Amazon's style rules.
- **You do:** spot the brands on your calls, sell it, read every word before it goes to the brand.
- **Honest risk:** results are slow to prove, so never promise a ranking or a sales number. Health and safety claims are the brand's to make; never invent one. If you also resell the brand, be clear which hat you are wearing. Edits in a brand's Seller Central are your own clicks, never a bot.
- **First step this week:** on your next 10 brand calls, note which brands have weak listings, and offer one a free fix of their best seller.

## 6. Rank-and-rent lead generation

**What it is.** You build a small website for one trade in one city, put a tracked phone number on it, get it to rank, and rent the calls to one contractor for a flat fee. You own the site and the number; the phone call is the product. If the contractor stops paying, the calls go to the next one.

**The job-value floor.** Pick niches where one job is worth thousands of dollars (our rule of thumb: an average job of $2,000 or more, like sewer line repair, foundation repair or standby generators). A contractor gladly pays rent when one job covers it many times over; a $150 job can't carry the rent.

**Rent first, then rank (our pattern):**
1. Test demand cheaply: a one-page site and a small paid ad budget that you set, with the tracked number.
2. Give a contractor the first few leads free.
3. Then a flat weekly or monthly rent, paid by card.
4. Build the full site, the slow organic part, only for trade-and-city combos that produced calls. Most test combos are meant to die.

- **Start cost:** a domain at cost (about $10 to $11 a year for a .com through Cloudflare Registrar, which charges the registry price with no markup; check [cloudflare.com/products/registrar](https://www.cloudflare.com/products/registrar/)), free static hosting on Cloudflare Pages, call tracking (CallRail starts around $45 to $55 a month in 2026 reviews; check callrail.com), and an ad test budget you choose.
- **Hours a week:** 4 to 8.
- **AI does:** niche and city research, the site and its pages, rank checks, the call log, invoice drafts.
- **You do:** buy the domain and number on your own accounts, call contractors and close them, set up ad accounts by hand (no AI in ad consoles), handle the money.
- **Honest risk:** slow; expect several near-dead months before organic ranking pays. The contractor is the weak link: track how many leads each one closes and swap a partner who doesn't answer or close. Legal: TCPA for any texting, California's all-party consent for call recording, a clear note on the site that you are a referral service and not the contractor. Never fake reviews, licenses, addresses or photos. Advisory only; talk to a lawyer before taking money in a licensed trade.
- **First step this week:** pick 3 high-ticket trades, look each one up in Google Maps in your city (our rule: 10 to 15 businesses showing means real demand), then call 5 contractors and ask one question: "Could you take on more jobs next month?"

## 7. eBay and Walmart reselling

**What it is.** eBay: buy underpriced items (clearance, returns, estate sales) and resell them, or sell your Amazon leftovers and returns. Walmart Marketplace: a second channel for products you already sell on Amazon, once you have a track record.

- **Start cost:** a few hundred dollars of stock to learn eBay (our estimate). Walmart reviews each seller application and checks the business details ([Walmart Marketplace guides, 2026](https://www.pricefy.io/articles/how-to-sell-on-walmart); check Walmart's own seller pages).
- **Hours a week:** 5 to 10 for eBay; photos, packing and shipping are the time.
- **AI does:** price research from sold listings, titles and descriptions, condition notes, a simple profit sheet.
- **You do:** sourcing, photos, packing, shipping, sending buyer replies.
- **Honest risk:** hand work that does not scale well around a day job; fees change (check eBay's selling fees page before pricing); returns; never sell a brand-name item you cannot prove is genuine.
- **First step this week:** list 10 things from your own home on eBay with Claude writing the listings, and learn the fees and shipping on items that cost you nothing.

## 8. Options income

**What it is.** Selling cash-secured puts (the wheel): you are paid up front for agreeing to buy 100 shares at a set price. See `amazon/amazon-vs-options-california.md` for the honest math and the `options/` folder for the practice kit.

- **Start cost:** no monthly cost at a commission-free broker; one put ties up the strike price x 100 in cash, so a $5K to $10K account holds one or two positions; strike x 100 at most 30 percent of the account ($1,500 at 5K, $3,000 at 10K), which means strikes of $15 or less at 5K and $30 or less at 10K.
- **Hours a week:** 1 to 3 once your rules are set.
- **AI does:** the paper-trading log, checks each trade against your written rules, the math, tax notes.
- **You do:** every trade, by your own hand, in your broker's app.
- **Honest risk:** small steady credits and a rare big loss; one bad drop can erase months of credits and tie all your money up in one stock. Your phone skill does not help here.
- **First step this week:** open a paper (practice) account and sell one put on paper.

## 9. Bookkeeping clean-up for small online sellers

**What it is.** Many small Amazon and eBay sellers have messy books: Amazon payouts never split into sales, fees and refunds; no cost of goods. Offer a monthly clean-up: every payout broken out, every bank line given its category, cost of goods from their invoices, a one-page profit report.

- **Start cost:** your Claude plan. A bookkeeping course is worth it before you charge anyone.
- **Hours a week:** 3 to 6.
- **AI does:** reads payout reports and bank exports the client gives you, drafts every entry with its reason, builds the report.
- **You do:** learn the basics first, review every entry, and let the client (or their CPA) approve. You never move money or touch a tax filing.
- **Honest risk:** mistakes in someone else's books cost them money and trust; you need real knowledge, not just the AI. Client statements hold private data: agree in writing how files are shared, and never paste account numbers into an AI chat.
- **First step this week:** do your own books for this business first (`amazon/04-money-and-mistakes.md`). Sell it only after three clean months of your own.

## What every one of these has in common

- **The AI drafts, you decide.** No AI sends a message to a customer, moves money, or clicks inside an ad console or Seller Central.
- **One result per job.** Each process ends with a named finished result (a report sent, a client signed, an order confirmed), not a to-do left hanging.
- **Build it once, then copy it.** The second client should take an afternoon, not a week.
