# Money and mistakes

## What Amazon takes

Check every number in Amazon's free Revenue Calculator or Seller Central's fee pages before you buy. Fees change often: Amazon announced its 2027 changes on 2026-11-05.

| Fee | Roughly | When |
|---|---|---|
| Referral fee | 15% of the sale price in many categories, 3% to 45% by category, $0.30 minimum in most ([sell.amazon.com/pricing](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing), checked 2026-11-12) | Every sale |
| FBA fulfillment fee | Set by the item's size, weight and price. Check Amazon's FBA fee page | Every unit Amazon ships |
| Fuel and logistics surcharge | 3.5% on top of FBA fees since 2026-04-17; Amazon kept it for 2027 (see sources) | Every unit Amazon ships |
| Monthly storage | Per cubic foot, higher October to December | Every month the stock sits |
| Aged inventory surcharge | Extra monthly charge from 181 days in a warehouse (2026 guides). From 2027-02-01 it merges into one age-based storage rate | Stock that does not sell |
| Low-inventory fee | When a product runs below about 28 days of supply | Running too lean |
| Inbound placement and defect fees | For how your shipment is split across warehouses, and for late, misrouted or missing units | Every shipment in |
| Professional plan | $39.99 a month ([sell.amazon.com/pricing](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing), checked 2026-11-12) | Always |

Coming in 2027 (Amazon's announcement of 2026-11-05, as reported by [Novadata](https://novadata.io/resources/news/amazon-2027-fba-fee-changes-us), 2026-11-06): FBA fulfillment fees rise about $0.19 a unit on average from 2027-01-15, and storage and aged charges merge on 2027-02-01. Stock that sells inside six months should pay the same or less; old stock pays more.

The two that surprise beginners: storage and aged fees punish buying too much, and the low-inventory fee punishes running out. Order sensible amounts and reorder on time.

## How the cash moves

1. You pay the supplier. First orders are usually paid up front; payment terms (pay in 30 days) come after a few orders.
2. The goods go to your prep center, which labels them and sends them into Amazon.
3. A customer buys. Amazon pays out your balance on a regular cycle (often about every two weeks; Seller Central shows yours).

Money you spend today usually comes back over one to three months as units sell. Plan for it to be tied up that long, and never spend the cash a reorder needs.

## Sales tax

Amazon is a marketplace facilitator: it collects and pays sales tax on the sales it handles for you. You still give suppliers a resale certificate so they don't charge you tax on inventory (see `00-setup.md`, step 6).

## Books in ten minutes a month

- One business bank account and card. Nothing personal goes through it.
- Keep **every supplier invoice**, forever. You need them for taxes, to get approved in restricted brands and categories, and to defend your account if a brand ever complains.
- Once a month, download the payments report from Seller Central (Claude will tell you which one) and hand it to Claude. It totals sales, Amazon fees, refunds and inventory bought into one simple sheet. Once you have more than a few brands, move to QuickBooks or a similar bookkeeping tool.
- **Cost of goods** is what you paid for the units you sold, not what you bought this month. Track landed cost per unit for every order so this number is right.
- Set aside tax money every month and pay quarterly estimates (see `amazon-vs-options-california.md`). A CPA at tax time is worth it in year one.

## Mistakes that cost real sellers real money

All of these happened in a working wholesale business. Each one has a check in this kit now.

1. **Trusting a screen without reading the chart.** A second look at one approved buy list rejected about two thirds of the lines that software had passed. Read the Keepa chart yourself, every time.
2. **Buying into a brand you can't win.** Private label, one seller, or an agency-run brand: you will never get the Buy Box. Two or more third-party FBA sellers, or skip it.
3. **Bad timing on seasonal goods.** Heaters arrived in March. It takes about 70 days from order to sellable.
4. **Sizing on the whole listing or on the brand's minimum.** Size on your share. One brand's minimums would have bought years of stock.
5. **Pack-size mistakes.** A 10-pack costed as a single unit made one product look 4 to 5 times more profitable than it was. Any margin over 40% gets its pack size checked.
6. **Misreading the price.** A short dip made a strong seller look dead; a used Buy Box was read as new. Read the end of the chart and check that the price is for new.
7. **Missing price breaks.** Bought at the "10+" price when "20+" was cheaper.
8. **Counting a cost twice.** A tariff was added on top of a price that already included it. Know what your quoted price includes.
9. **Not filing for lost and damaged units.** Amazon loses and damages stock, and the claim windows are short; once a window closes, that money is gone. Check monthly.
10. **Too much of something slow.** Dead stock ties up cash and Amazon charges more every month it sits. When in doubt, buy less and reorder.
11. **Matching on a model number alone.** One model number covered several colors and pack sizes. Match on the UPC, or on the model plus size, color and pack.
12. **A chase that never happened.** Orders sat "ready" and unsent, and sent orders were never confirmed. An order is done when the brand confirms it, not when it is drafted.

Also: find out you're approved to sell an item **before** building the order, and open price lists the day they arrive.
