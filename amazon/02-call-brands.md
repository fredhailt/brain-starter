# Calling brands

This is where a wholesale business is won or lost, and it is the part you are already good at. Run `/brand-call` before and after every call.

## Fitting calls around a day job

- Brands answer in **their** business hours. Block two or three call windows a week: a lunch hour, an early morning for East Coast brands, a day off.
- Use a separate business number (a second-line app on your phone works), never the work phone or work email.
- Batch the prep: run `/brand-call` on a whole call window's brands the night before.

## Find the right door (public sources only)

- The brand's **official website** only (not an Amazon or marketplace page): its contact, wholesale, dealer or retailer pages. If something is unclear, mark it "not found" rather than guessing.
- On the phone, ask the front desk for the **sales manager, wholesale or dealer manager, or e-commerce manager**. At a small brand, ask for the owner.
- **Never the work ZoomInfo, the work CRM or any company data.** It is almost certainly against your employer's rules, and it puts your job at risk for a side business.

## Three minutes before you dial

1. Pick one word for their line (for example "fishing tools").
2. Search them on Amazon. Are they running Sponsored ads? Is anyone selling **below their own website price** (breaking MAP, which brands hate)? Anything out of stock? Stars and review count?
3. Size them: their top five products' "bought in past month" x price is roughly their monthly Amazon sales.

## The call, in seven steps

1. **Front desk:** ask for the role above.
2. **Opener:** who you are, an e-commerce company, and that you want to carry their line.
3. **Why them:** one honest sentence about their product.
4. **What you noticed:** something from your three-minute check (out of stock, sellers under their price).
5. **What you bring:** the three promises below.
6. **Partnership:** you want to grow their line on Amazon over time, not flip it once.
7. **The ask:** "What's the best next step to get an account set up?" Get the person's name, title, email and direct line, and ask: do they have a MAP policy, what is the opening minimum, and do they sell on Amazon themselves?

## Three promises, and only ones you can keep

- **Protect their price.** You never sell below MAP.
- **Keep their best sellers in stock** with clean listings.
- **Easy to work with.** You prepay (wire or card, no terms), order full case packs, hit their minimums, ship to Amazon's warehouses (FBA), never drop ship, and answer fast.

Never promise a sales number you cannot buy. "I'll open with full cases and grow with reorders" is honest and enough.

## Words to use and avoid

- Say **"e-commerce company"** first, not "Amazon reseller".
- Never say "bulk" or "small order", and never open with "do you have two minutes?"
- Call mid-morning or mid-afternoon in their time zone, Tuesday to Thursday when you can. No voicemail until the third try.

## The usual objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We don't sell to Amazon sellers." | Agree most are a problem, then: you hold MAP, you prepay, you protect the listing. Offer to email your info |
| "We already have enough sellers." | You don't compete on price; you hold MAP and keep it in stock |
| "We're distributor only." | Thank them and close politely. You buy brand-direct only |
| "You need a physical store." | Ask whether that is firm |
| "Just email me." | Confirm the address and who handles new accounts |
| "We're not opening accounts." | Ask when to check back, and put that date in the tracker |
| "Who else do you carry?" | Partners' details stay private, the same way theirs would |
| "What price would you sell at?" | Never below MAP |
| "Do you need terms?" | No, you prepay |
| "Can you drop ship?" | No, you ship to Amazon (FBA) |
| "Send a resale certificate, tax ID, business license." | Yes, with the application |
| Exclusivity, special pricing, contracts | Never promise any of these on the call. Say you will review it and come back |

## What they will ask for

Your legal or business name, how the business is set up, your EIN (never your Social Security number), billing and ship-to addresses, phone, email, and a resale certificate (see `00-setup.md`, step 6). Trade references: "N/A, I prepay."

An application fee, a request for your Social Security number, or a contract you have not read: stop and think before you agree. Claude can read a contract with you and flag what to ask about; it is not a lawyer.

## Every call ends with one result

Log exactly one of these in `tracker/brands.csv`:

| Result | Next step |
|---|---|
| PRICE LIST | Run `/first-order` on it the day it arrives |
| APPLICATION | Fill it in, you sign it, ask for the price list |
| YES | Write down the terms and the next step |
| MAYBE | The date they gave to call back |
| NO | The reason (no Amazon sellers, distributor only, stores only, a hard no) |
| NO ANSWER | Three touches, then dormant, and try again in 3 months |

## After the call

- The same day: a short email with what they asked for and one next step. One topic, one ask, friendly. **Never put a price, discount, your costs or your margins in an email.** Claude drafts it; you read it, edit it and send it.
- No reply: nudge on day 3, day 7 and day 14, then mark it dormant. Three unanswered touches is the limit.
- They replied, then went quiet: every 5 working days. A live thread: reply within 1 working day.
- Account open but no price list yet: ask for it once a week until it comes.
- If their numbers don't work for you, still send a friendly "not right now" email. Brands remember who was polite.
