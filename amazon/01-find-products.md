# Finding products worth calling about

These rules come from a working Amazon wholesale business (our house rules, 2026), with a few made stricter for a beginner (marked). `/product-check` reads this page, so change a number here and the check changes with it.

## Judge by monthly profit, not by margin

Any category works if it clears the kills below. The question is how much a product makes **per month on your realistic share of sales**, not how pretty the margin percent looks.

A brand is worth calling when **3 or more of its products pass and together they make $300 or more a month on your share** (house rule). One good product is not a brand worth opening.

## The gates

| Gate | Pass | Kill |
|---|---|---|
| Who sells it | 2 or more third-party FBA sellers share the Buy Box | The brand is the only seller, it is private label, there has been about 1 seller in 90 days, or an agency runs the brand on Amazon |
| Amazon itself | Amazon has not sold it in the last year. In stock under 15% of the year and out of stock today is a warning: read the chart and buy smaller | Amazon in stock today, or in stock 15% or more of the year (stricter for a beginner) |
| Sales | 3 or more a month to count at all | Under 1 sales-rank drop a month: dead |
| Crowd | 10 or fewer sellers and not climbing | 15 or more sellers (stricter for a beginner), or new sellers piling on in the last 30 days while the price slides (a price war) |
| Price | Buy Box now at 90% or more of its 90-day average; flat, steady lines are best | A staircase down |
| History | Listing at least 90 days old | Under 90 days: too little history to judge |
| Margin after referral, FBA, freight and prep | **Beginner floor: 15%** (a business with pallet freight and scale goes lower; you do not have that cushion) | Over 40%: check the pack size before believing it. Over 55%: never counts, it is almost always a pack-size mistake |
| What it is | Buy Box around $30 to $40 or more, small, well packaged | Anything eaten or swallowed (food, supplements). Children's products, cosmetics and lithium batteries until you carry insurance (see `00-setup.md`, step 10). Liquids, glass, ceramic, clothing and sized goods: strong caution (breakage and returns) |
| Restrictions | You are approved to sell the brand and category | Restricted and not approved. Hazmat (dangerous goods) for a beginner. Patented or Brand Registry brands without a written reseller authorization |

## Reading a Keepa chart (do this before trusting any number)

1. Look at the 3-month view, then the 1-year view, then the Statistic panel. **Read the end of the chart, not the average.**
2. Price: flat, MAP-like lines are best. A staircase down is a kill.
3. Amazon's own line (orange on Keepa): in stock today, or across 15% or more of the year, is a kill.
4. A "no data" banner means the listing is dead now.
5. For products with sizes or colors (a variation family), set the range to All and read the sales rank from before the family was split.
6. Check the price is for **new**. A used offer holding the Buy Box makes a product look cheaper or deader than it is.
7. Reviews are context only.
8. Judge the margin at today's price, not a past high.
9. A big or heavy item with no FBA sellers at all: kill.

A human looks at the chart every time. Software can screen a thousand products; the chart is where a screen is wrong (see mistake 1 in `04-money-and-mistakes.md`).

## Your share of sales

Your share is roughly **monthly sales / (sellers sharing the Buy Box + 1)**.

- A seller "shares the box" when they win it at least 5% of the time over the last 30 days.
- Never assume more than an even split.
- **Halve it if Amazon wins the box.**

## Matching a brand's item to the right listing

- Match on the **UPC** (the barcode number) first.
- No UPC: match on the brand's model number **plus** size, color and pack count. A model number alone is not unique; one number can cover several colors or pack sizes.
- Never trust a title match alone.

## Where leads come from

- **Competitor storefronts.** Open a seller who carries brands like the ones you want, list their brands, and run each brand's top products through `/product-check`. A storefront with 3 or more brands that pass is a gold mine; check the other sellers on those listings next.
- **Brands you already know and use.**
- **Trade-show exhibitor lists** in a category you like.

Finding brands is the easy part; software can find a thousand. **Calling them is the job**, and that is where most people stop.
